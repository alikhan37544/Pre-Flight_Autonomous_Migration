The problem

A migration estimate is a promise. The inventory is the proof.

The vendor estimate is not the risk by itself. The risk is the scope hidden underneath it: files that were never classified, features nobody traced, fields nobody disposed, and questions nobody owns.

Estimate-moving scope

Six to eight weeks is a vendor estimate for APD product conversion — not a full migration estimate. It is a useful anchor. It is not an inventory of your estate.

01

What exists?

Every file, hash, parser status, and the four that cannot be parsed — listed, not dropped.

02

Which feature?

Cross-artefact links from PCF to entity, typelist, template, Gosu, and tests.

03

What is disposed?

An explicit source-to-target disposition, or a named gap. Never a silent rename.

04

What is unknown?

Owner, impact range, and the one question that would close the gap.

APD types

24-character type limit → compile errors

APD limits type names to 24 characters. A longer name can be valid in source and still fail when endpoint generation compiles the generated code. The documented correction is a codegen nameOverride with the installed name.

The evidence model

A claim is only as strong as its locator, its label, and its open question.

PreFlight does not store “a finding.” It stores a claim with a source pointer, an excerpt hash, a method, a confidence label, and — where the evidence runs out — an assumption with an owner.

Evidence before assertion

Anatomy of a claim

{ "evidence_id": "EV-0001", "path": "config/web/pcf/policy/PAVehicleDV.pcf", "locator": "xpath /PCF/Screen/TypeKeyInput[1]:4", "excerpt": "<TypeKeyInput id=\"VehicleType\" …/>", "excerpt_sha": "d76bf9908b1f…", "method": "xml_xpath", "confidence": "parsed" }

Why the hash matters

The excerpt is not decorative. It is re-read from the snapshot and hashed; a fabricated or drifting excerpt fails the locator-support audit.

What the record does not prove

  • That the excerpt is semantically sufficient.
  • That the parser understood every construct.
  • That the decision is correct — only that it is inspectable.

Claim-strength ladder

A label caps what may be inferred.

verified-id12Public standard ID/title verified; body may be gated.
verified-policy3Public policy or hardening guidance; no rule ID claimed.
verified-doc3Documented mechanism, e.g. the 24-character APD limit.
secondary3Credible secondary source, e.g. an experience report.
heuristic3PreFlight pattern; explicitly not a Cloud Standard.

Counts are the 24 advisory findings in R-DEMO-001. The separate SBT readiness flag is not one of the 24.

The packet hash covers the rendered packet HTML (hash fields normalized); packet_json separately covers canonical packet JSON; content digests bind findings, mappings, and evidence. The audit head covers the append-only event chain. Verification is local and tamper-evident — not non-repudiation.

Reviewer question

If this evidence disappeared, which decision would become unsafe? If the answer is “none,” the record is not carrying its weight.

Chain of custody Capture → link → label → seal. Read-only file; excerpt and SHA-256 at capture · evidence ID attached to a feature, finding, or gap · claim strength caps what may be inferred · packet hash and audit head make the artifact reviewable.

This is the trust model. Read the EV-0001 card: path, xpath plus line 4, excerpt, excerpt hash, method, confidence. “If you cannot point to it, it is not a finding. It is an assumption.”

Explain claim strength carefully: “verified-id means the ID and title are verified in public material. It does not mean we have the gated standard body. Heuristic means this is our pattern, not Guidewire’s.”

Then the assumption register: territory is a real field in the entity, but no rating table exists in the snapshot. The output is not a zero. It is A-01 with an owner, a heuristic range, and a question. The audit chain is hash-linked but local; it is tamper-evident, not non-repudiation.
